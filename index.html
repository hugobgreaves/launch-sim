🪐 Educational model. Planet and Moon positions are real (astronomy-engine ephemerides, computed offline in your browser; checked against NASA JPL Horizons). Trajectories are simplified patched conics: a single Lambert (two-body) arc between Earth and the target with no planetary gravity during the transfer, no mid-course corrections and no deep-space manoeuvres; Earth launch, orbital refuelling and propellant limits are not modelled (the mission starts at the injection burn from a 250 km Earth orbit). Mars entry, descent and landing and the lunar orbit/descent are illustrative timelines, not SpaceX flight data. The Moon profile flies to a low 100 km lunar orbit (real Starship HLS plans use a high near-rectilinear halo orbit). Window dates match NASA's Mars mission design handbook (TM-2010-216764) to within ~1–2 days.

⚠️ Educational visualisation only. Speeds, altitudes and times are approximate, rounded public (encyclopedia-level) figures for generic vehicle classes, run through simplified physics (non-rotating spherical Earth, no drag or weather). Named vehicles show rounded public figures (top speed, range, altitude); the simulated flight uses the simplified model for that vehicle type, so it won't match any real flight. Ranges are enforced: beyond a vehicle's cited max range the flight stops short. Vehicle lists follow simple public alliance groupings for the launch country.
